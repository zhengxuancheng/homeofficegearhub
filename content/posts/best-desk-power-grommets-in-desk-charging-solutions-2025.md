Most standing desks have a grommet hole somewhere in the desktop. Usually it is plugged with a cheap plastic cap that you ignore until you start thinking about cable management. That hole is actually the best path for running power up from under the desk. A power grommet replaces the cap with outlets and USB ports mounted flush into the desktop. No cables draped over the edge. No reaching underneath.

We installed 7 units in a standing desk with a 2-inch bamboo top and tested outlet accessibility with various plug sizes, USB charging speed across three devices, and wireless charging where applicable. The results are straightforward: some of these are worth the money, some are overpriced, and a few are genuinely useful.

## What to Look For

### Grommet Hole Compatibility

Standard grommet holes are 2 to 3 inches in diameter. Measure your desk hole before buying. Many grommets come with adapter rings to fit multiple hole sizes. If your desk has no grommet hole, you can drill one with a hole saw, but that is not reversible. Some power grommets are designed to sit on top of the desk instead of inside the hole.

### Outlet Configuration

Basic units offer one AC outlet. Better units offer two or three plus USB-A and USB-C ports. The critical detail is outlet orientation. If the outlets face upward, you see the plugs from above. If they face sideways or forward, the plugs hide behind the grommet housing. Side-facing outlets also work better with bulky wall warts that block adjacent outlets.

### USB Charging Speed

USB-A ports on desk grommets typically deliver 2.4A shared across both ports. USB-C should deliver at least 20W for phone charging and 65W or more for laptops. Check the specs carefully. Many grommets advertise "fast charging" but deliver only 10-15W, which is barely faster than a standard charger.

### Desk Thickness Compatibility

Grommet housings have a maximum desk thickness they fit. A 2-inch housing cannot handle a 3-inch thick butcher block desk. Most units fit standard 1.5-inch to 2.5-inch desktops. If you have a thick custom desk, look for units with extendable housings.

## FAQ

### Do I need a desk power grommet?

You need one if you are tired of cables running over the edge of your desk or reaching under the desk to plug things in. A grommet brings power to the desktop surface where you can reach it. If your current setup works fine with an under-desk power strip, a grommet is not necessary. If you frequently plug and unplug devices on your desktop, it makes a difference.

### Will a power grommet fit my standing desk?

Most standing desks have a grommet hole, usually on the back left or back right corner. Measure the diameter first. Standard sizes are 2 inches (50mm) and 3 inches (75mm). Many grommets come with adapter rings to fit multiple sizes. If your desk has no grommet hole, you can drill one with a hole saw or use a desktop power strip instead.

### Can I install a grommet in a drill hole?

Yes, that is the standard installation method. Use a hole saw in a drill to cut a clean hole. Go slowly to avoid splintering the top surface. For laminate desks, score the laminate first with a utility knife before drilling. The grommet retaining ring covers the raw edge, so the cut does not need to be perfect.

### Why is USB-C power delivery important?

USB-C Power Delivery allows a single port to charge a phone, tablet, and laptop at different speeds. A 20W USB-C port fast-charges an iPhone but is too slow for a MacBook. A 65W USB-C port charges a MacBook Pro at full speed and fast-charges an iPhone. If you want to eliminate a separate laptop charger, get a grommet with 60W+ USB-C PD.

### Will the pop-up mechanism wear out?

Gas-strut pop-up mechanisms are rated for 10,000+ cycles. Spring-loaded mechanisms also last years with normal use. The most common failure point is the locking mechanism, not the lift. Look for a grommet with a mechanical latch rather than a friction fit. Avoid units that require significant force to push down and lock.
