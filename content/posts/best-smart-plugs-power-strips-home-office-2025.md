A monitor that powers down completely when you leave. A space heater that shuts off if no one's been in the room for an hour. ## What to Look For in a Smart Plug or Power Strip

### Wireless Protocol: Wi-Fi vs. Thread vs. Zigbee

Three main protocols dominate the 2025 smart plug market. **Wi-Fi direct** plugs connect to your existing router without a hub — easiest setup of any option. You plug it in, install the app, and it's on your network in two minutes. The tradeoff: every Wi-Fi plug adds another device to your router's load. Beyond 20-30 Wi-Fi plugs, network congestion becomes noticeable, especially on older routers without Wi-Fi 6. **Thread** plugs use a mesh network that improves as you add more Thread devices, with lower latency and better reliability than Wi-Fi in crowded network environments. They require a Thread border router (Apple HomePod, Google Nest Hub, or Amazon Echo with Thread). **Zigbee** plugs need a dedicated hub (Amazon Echo Plus, Hubitat, SmartThings) but offer excellent reliability and very low power consumption. For most home office setups with 3-8 smart plugs, Wi-Fi direct is fine. For larger installations, Thread is the better long-term choice.

### Energy Monitoring

The ability to track how much power your devices consume is useful beyond novelty. Energy-monitoring plugs report real-time wattage, daily and monthly kWh totals, and estimated cost. This helps identify devices that draw power when idle (monitors, printers, chargers) and quantify the savings from shutting them down. The accuracy varies: good plugs report within 1-2% of a reference meter; poor ones can be off by 10% or more. Look for plugs that report at least once per minute for real-time monitoring, and that store historical data in the cloud so you can see patterns over days and weeks.

### Away Mode and Vacation Scheduling

A smart plug that turns a lamp on and off randomly while you're on vacation is the best security device you can buy. Good plugs support randomized scheduling — turns on at sunset, off at 10pm, but varies the timing by 10-30 minutes each day to avoid the programmed-looking pattern that burglars recognize. Away mode should work without keeping your phone connected, and should survive a power outage with the schedule saved in the device memory (not just in the cloud). Some plugs lose their schedule after a power cut and sit in whatever state they default to — usually off — which defeats the purpose.

### Physical Size and Outlet Clearance

Many smart plugs are bulky. The relay mechanism, Wi-Fi antenna, and internal power supply take up space, and a standard smart plug can block the adjacent outlet completely. Compact models exist that fit two per duplex outlet. For power strips, the spacing between outlets matters — wall-wart power adapters need extra space. If you're plugging a smart plug into a power strip, the smart plug's depth can also block the adjacent strip outlet. Measure before you buy.

### Response Time and App Quality

A smart plug that takes 5 seconds to respond to a voice command is frustrating. Good plugs respond in under 1 second locally (on the same network) and under 2 seconds through a cloud roundtrip. The app matters more than you'd expect — a good app lets you set schedules, view energy data, create groups, and integrate with Alexa/Google Home/Siri without navigating through layers of menus and ads. Some brands (Kasa, Eve) are excellent. Others (no-name brands from Amazon) have terrible apps full of subscription upsells and slow loading times.

## FAQ

### Do smart plugs use power when turned off?

Yes, but very little. A Wi-Fi smart plug draws 0.5-2 watts in standby to maintain its network connection. Over a year, that's roughly 5-17 kWh — about $1-$3 depending on your electricity rate. Thread-based plugs draw even less (0.1-0.5 watts). The energy savings from turning off devices on a schedule far outweighs the standby draw of the plug itself. For example, turning off a monitor that draws 30W in standby for 16 hours a day saves about 175 kWh/year — roughly \$25 — while the plug itself costs \$1-2 to run.

### Can I use a smart plug with a space heater?

You can, but be careful. Most smart plugs are rated for 15A (1800W at 120V), which covers most space heaters (1500W). The problem is that space heaters draw high current for extended periods, and cheap relays can overheat or weld shut over time. Use a known-brand plug (Kasa, Eve, Meross) with a proper 15A relay. Check the plug's temperature periodically during the first few uses. Never put a space heater on a smart plug with a 10A rating, and always ensure the plug is fully inserted with no loose connection. Some smart plug manufacturers explicitly recommend against space heater use regardless of rating — check the documentation.

### What's the difference between a smart plug and a smart power strip?

A smart plug controls one outlet. A smart power strip controls multiple outlets individually or as a group. The main advantage of a smart strip is reducing Wi-Fi congestion — one device on your network instead of three or six. Smart strips also include surge protection (most do) and often have always-on outlets for devices that shouldn't be switched off. The tradeoff is physical placement — a plug goes where you need it, while a strip needs to be placed and the connected devices have to reach it. For a desk setup, a smart strip is usually cleaner.

### Do I need a hub for smart plugs?

It depends on the protocol. Wi-Fi smart plugs (Kasa, Meross, Amazon, GHome) work without a hub — they connect directly to your router. Thread plugs (Eve, Wemo) need a Thread border router, which is built into many recent smart speakers (HomePod mini, Apple TV 4K, Echo fourth-gen and later, Nest Hub Max). Zigbee plugs need a Zigbee hub. For most home offices with 2-8 plugs, Wi-Fi is the simplest choice. For homes with 20+ devices, Thread or a hub-based system will be more reliable.

### Can smart plugs work without internet?

Local control (on the same Wi-Fi network) usually works without internet. If your ISP goes down, you can still turn a Kasa plug on and off from the app on your phone as long as both devices are on the same local network. Cloud features (voice control through Alexa, remote access from outside your home) require internet. Schedules are usually stored on the plug itself and run whether the internet is available or not — though some budget plugs lose scheduling after a power outage if the configuration wasn't synced to the device.

### How secure are smart plugs?

Smart plugs are IoT devices with the security implications that come with that category. The main risk is that a compromised smart plug becomes an entry point into your home network. Buy from established brands (Kasa, Eve, Belkin, Meross) that provide firmware updates. Use a separate IoT network (most modern routers support this) to isolate smart plugs from your computers and phones. The Meross MSS310 using ESP32 is an interesting case — the chip is well-supported by secure third-party firmware, but the stock firmware's security is average. For privacy-sensitive users, the Eve Energy with local-only data and no cloud dependency is the most secure option.
